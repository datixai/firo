The problem

Today's fire monitoring is too slow

Forest fires in Pakistan's north and in AJK are growing more frequent and more severe. The tools used to find them were not built for early warning.

Satellites arrive late

Revisit delays, cloud cover and coarse resolution mean small fires often go unseen until they have already grown.

Watchtowers don't scale

Towers need people watching around the clock and are hard to place across steep, remote mountain forest.

Reports come too late

Calls from the public usually arrive once smoke is visible from a village, when the best time to act has passed.

How it works

From camera to alert in seconds

All the heavy work happens on the device in the forest. Only a few bytes of results travel over the network.

Capture

A tower-mounted camera photographs the forest at regular intervals.

Classify on-device

A small edge computer runs an optimised AI model that decides fire or no fire in under a second.

Sync results

Only the result, confidence, location and time are sent to the cloud. No images are uploaded.

Alert & respond

The live dashboard lights up and staff forward the alert on WhatsApp with one tap.

Why FIRO

Built for remote forests

Low cost, low power and low bandwidth, so it can go where traditional systems can't.

On-device AI

AI models with 97.5% accuracy run on the device itself, so detection keeps working when the connection is weak.

Live monitoring dashboard

Every camera on a satellite map, colour-coded by status, with a real-time alert log.

Instant WhatsApp alerts

Alerts go to Forest Department staff with location, map link and time, in English and Urdu.

Public reporting

Anyone can report smoke or fire with location and a photo. Reports go straight to the response team.

Minimal bandwidth

Compact metadata instead of images keeps costs down on mobile networks.

Works at night

Detected every real night-time forest fire it was tested on near Khuiratta and Nakyal, AJK.
